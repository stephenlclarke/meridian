## Original visible hull

`visual.bin` encodes all 7,236 original detailed triangles, in original corner order, at exactly 40000/3411 metres per source unit followed by f32 encoding. It retains every per-face material colour, emissive flag, texture index and UV, with derived flat normals. `tools/encode_mule_visual.py --check` reconstructs those fields from the authenticated resource. The detailed model is not replaced by its collision envelope.

Only the three diffuse RGBA maps named by this Mule resource are added. Their bytes come from immutable revision `a11fe16924d87d06021816f9037952afcbac08db`, authenticated by the original texture manifest. `VISUAL-PROVENANCE.json`, `TEXTURE-SOURCE.json` and the existing MIT notice retain their provenance. No other hull, texture set, live checkout or player data is imported. Base pixels remain unchanged; repeated UVs, filtered sampling and linear-light box mips preserve the authored maps. Their baked relief is not lit a second time. Material tint is applied in linear light; opaque materials remain opaque.

## Same rounded contact surface

The render input shares the collision presentation's complete immutable 34-piece support data. It must equal the authenticated `MuleHullSource` and revision. For each piece, a bounded incremental boundary builder uses only those original source points. It validates every original point against every outward facet, an interior anchor, closed oppositely oriented edge pairs and Euler closure. Work is capped at sixteen million point/facet visits per piece. Source coordinates are bounded to 64 metres and the entire upload to `2 * 5344 - 4 * 34` facets. This preparation runs once on the owning lane when a genuine Mule first needs the pass; no hull algorithm was executed by the author.

Let `H` be the constructed convex boundary, `c` its interior anchor, `rho` the smallest anchor-to-facet distance, `R` the largest original-point distance from `c`, and `s` the largest original-point outward facet slack plus the explicit arithmetic guard. Every boundary vertex is an original source point, so `H` lies in their convex support. All original points lie within the halfspaces of `H` expanded by `s`. Scaling `H` about `c` by `1 + s/rho` contains their expanded halfspace intersection. The resulting Hausdorff bound is `s * R / rho`. Preparation rejects bounds above ten micrometres. Tests independently compare every piece's original and boundary supports over 124 nonzero integer directions. This is a bounded source consistency certificate, not a change to the physical core or the unresolved exact-original geometry qualification.

The shader intersects the spherical 0.02-metre offset analytically: offset facet planes, finite edge cylinders and vertex spheres. The ball is not tessellated into a larger bubble or replaced with an ellipsoid. Adding the same ball preserves the boundary certificate. Position encoding is checked at no more than four micrometres per coordinate, adding at most seven micrometres of Euclidean vertex error. Normals come from the f64 facets, rather than unstable f32 cross products of tiny edges. The geometric representation therefore stays within seventeen micrometres of the source-rounded support before shader arithmetic. Ten-micrometre edge-seam tolerance and a 0.2-millimetre strict-interior refusal guard are explicit. Actual shader arithmetic, silhouette coverage and GPU acceptance remain unmeasured and require the owning tests; the CPU certificate is not a claim of GPU bit identity.

## One faint union boundary

The pass sends one fullscreen triangle for the union. Bounding boxes only reject rays; they never define the visible surface. It selects the nearest incoming analytic surface across all pieces, rejects a camera/near plane inside the envelope and discards a surface strictly inside any piece. It applies alpha once with premultiplied blending and existing opaque depth, without writing depth. Overlapping parts cannot accumulate separate translucent layers. An uncertified boundary disables shimmer and retains the original detailed solid; it does not choose another envelope.

Opacity is capped at 0.018, with fourth-power grazing fade, full close strength through 32 metres and squared fade to zero at 64 metres. A restrained 0.90-to-1.00 modulation uses the immutable simulation tick. The front bank applies at local `z >= 0`, rear otherwise. The snapshot fractions must match the source contract's 240.00-unit banks. Depletion hides shimmer without changing contact geometry. The controlled station and other actual scene primitives share the World observer frame rather than the normal chase offset; the observing pilot's own visible hull is omitted in this first-person view. The scenario observer is the frozen actual pilot pose, so the existing sky view stays coherent. The camera's actual view basis is inverted for rays, including f32 quaternion residual; the renderer never repairs a World pose or changes aiming.

Pipeline, texture and geometry allocations are lazy and retained for this one surface. There are no per-frame GPU allocations, readbacks, waits or diagnostic output. Facet/work bounds and invisible refusal do not demonstrate acceptable frame rate. Memory, first preparation cost, close-view GPU time and native appearance remain unobserved.

## Validation

The encoder tests check face order, material and UV encoding, original texture and notice identity, and rejection of a changed resource. Renderer tests cover source boundaries, tightness, camera views, opaque mips, shader parsing and uniform layout. A separate Metal test uses the production shader with a labelled cube to check overlap, alpha, occlusion and distance fade. The controlled World docking checks exercise the original Mule mesh and the actual station. Their timings measure CPU work. GPU frame rate remains unmeasured.
